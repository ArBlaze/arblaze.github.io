COFFEE CONSUMER INSIGHTS
Arsalan Ahmed

Consumer research · 2023 survey data

What do coffee drinkers prefer?

A look at coffee habits, spending and taste preferences, based on 4,042 responses from the Great American Coffee Taste Test.

The main finding

Coffee D was the most popular choice, but preferences differed by group. It was much more popular among people who said they liked light roasts than among those who preferred dark roasts. A coffee brand could use this to plan which products to test with different customers.

Coffee D was the favourite, but most chose something else

Coffee D was the favourite for 36.7% of respondents who answered the question. The other choices were close: A received 21.7%, C 20.8%, and B 20.8%.

D had the largest share, but it was not a majority choice. Looking at the overall result alone would miss differences between consumers.

3,770 valid answers. 272 respondents did not answer.

People with different roast preferences chose different coffees

Among respondents who preferred light roasts, 53.5% chose Coffee D. This fell to 23.4% for medium-roast drinkers and 16.2% for dark-roast drinkers. Coffee B was the most common choice in the dark-roast group.

Roast preference Responses Coffee A Coffee B Coffee C Coffee D
Light 1,700 25.6% 10.6% 10.3% 53.5%
Medium 1,485 19.1% 27.9% 29.5% 23.4%

Percentages are within each group and may not total exactly 100% because of rounding. This comparison includes Light, Medium and Dark responses only.

A useful next step would be to test products with each group separately. The results do not tell us which coffee characteristic caused the difference: A–D identify different coffees, not controlled tests of roast level.

Coffee D and Coffee A had similar average ratings

Coffee D averaged 3.38 out of 5, compared with 3.31 for Coffee A. D received more high ratings, but it also received more low ratings than A. It was a stronger favourite for some respondents and less appealing to others.

Coffee A 3.31 49.3% 26.8% 3,789
Coffee B 3.07 35.5% 31.9% 3,773
Coffee C 3.06 36.2% 31.9% 3,766
Coffee D 3.38 54.5% 30.8% 3,764

Each average uses the available answers for that coffee. The number of responses differs slightly. Higher scores indicate greater preference.

This is why both questions matter: which coffee someone chooses as their favourite, and how much they like each coffee. The small difference in averages does not make D an obvious choice for everyone.

Most respondents drank coffee at home

91.8% said they drank coffee at home. Respondents could choose more than one location, so someone might drink coffee both at home and at a cafe.

An at-home sample pack could be worth testing with a similar audience. These results alone do not show whether people would buy it.

3,970 valid location answers. Two conflicting responses were excluded from this question.

Most reported monthly spending between $20 and $60

66.7% selected either the $20–$40 or $40–$60 monthly spending bracket.

In a separate question, 41.5% said they did not get good value when buying coffee at a cafe. It would be useful to ask what they bought, what they paid, and what would make it feel worth the price.

Monthly spending does not tell us the right price for one product. It gives some context for a more specific pricing study.

Spending: 3,511 answers. Cafe value: 3,500 answers. Dollar brackets are shown as supplied in the survey, without conversion to Canadian dollars.

What I would look at next

A product test with different coffee drinkers

I would recruit people with different roast preferences and ask them to try the same products under similar conditions. I would compare their ratings and favourite choices, then ask what they liked or disliked.

I would also test whether a sample pack appeals to people who drink coffee at home. For pricing, I would use a specific product and serving size rather than relying on general monthly spending.

These are suggested next steps. No follow-up product or pricing study has been carried out.

About the analysis

The analysis uses counts, percentages, average ratings and comparisons between groups. Missing answers are left out of each question’s calculation. The number of valid answers is shown with each result.

The original survey took place in October 2023. Participants volunteered for a tasting organised by James Hoffmann and Cometeer, so the results describe this sample. They should not be treated as a picture of all coffee drinkers or current Canadian preferences.
